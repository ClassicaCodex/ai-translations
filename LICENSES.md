Every translation is licensed as its source requires, and its header (`header.availability`) says so in full. `catalogue.csv` gives the licence and credit for each file in `translations/`, and `testimonia/catalogue.csv` for each file in `testimonia/`.

All 1,135 files in `testimonia/` are CC BY-SA 4.0. They come from Perseus and from Open Greek and Latin (First1KGreek, CSEL and the Patrologia Latina), the first two rows below. Each source edition's licence was checked: 1,038 by the edition's own header and 97 by its repository's statement.

The 111 whole works in `translations/` are CC BY-SA 4.0 too, from the same two rows: 70 from the Patrologia Latina, 38 from First1KGreek, 2 from CSEL and 1 from Perseus. Each source edition's licence was checked: 110 by the edition's own header, and the Perseus text, which has no statement of its own, by its repository's statement.

## Sources left out

Nothing here comes from a source licensed for non-commercial use only, or from a file marked not for distribution. The Geste corpus marks its Otinel transcriptions (manuscripts A, B and M) as working files not to be distributed, so the one cited Otinel (A) passage is not translated here.

## Copyright in AI text

Under current US law, text generated by AI without human authorship may not be protected by copyright. The licences above are given for what they cover: the source's terms, which apply to anything made from the source, and any rights there may be in the selection and arrangement.
